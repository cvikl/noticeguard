I looked through the whole case file before drafting anything, and I don't think a counter-notice is the right move here — not because of a technicality, but because the underlying facts don't support it. Here's what I found:

**The gap:**
- Your licence certificate (GA-CERT-08231) and receipt both say **Standard licence**.
- Licence Terms v3, §4.1: *"Standard licence permits use of the Work as background music in personal, non-commercial video content. Monetised use requires a Pro licence."*
- The ClipStream export shows the video **"Monetisation at publish: ON"** and monetised from 2025-09-10, and the track was used as background for the **entire 42:15 "full set"** — not a short clip.

So the licence you actually purchased explicitly does **not** cover the way the track was used. A Pro licence (GBP 89) would have.

**Why this matters for a counter-notice specifically:**
A DMCA counter-notification requires you to state, under penalty of perjury, a good-faith belief that the material was removed "as a result of mistake or misidentification." Here:
- There's no misidentification — it *is* Glasslight, correctly matched.
- There's arguably no mistake either — the removal traces back to a licence-tier mismatch (Standard vs. monetised use), which is a contractual/licensing issue between you and Glasswork Audio, not a false takedown.

Signing that statement as written wouldn't be accurate on the facts in front of me, and a false statement in a counter-notice carries real legal exposure (it's the one part of the DMCA process that's an actual sworn declaration, and the claimant can escalate to a court action within 10 business days if they contest it).
