A DMCA counter-notification requires you to state, **under penalty of perjury**, that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed "as a result of mistake or misidentification." Based on what's in these documents, this doesn't look like a misidentification — Northline Rights correctly identified the track, and the removal may well be *correct* under your licence's own terms, because the licence you actually hold doesn't cover the monetised use you made of it.

Filing a counter-notice on this basis carries real risk:
- A false perjury statement is a serious legal exposure on its own.
- It also consents to a US court's jurisdiction, and if Northline Rights/Glasswork pursued it, you'd be defending a case where your own licence certificate contradicts your "mistake" claim.
