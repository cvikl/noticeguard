## A note on the facts first

Before you send this, it's worth being clear-eyed about what actually happened, because it affects how strong your "mistake or misidentification" claim is:

- Your **Standard** licence (v3 terms, §4.1) only covered **non-commercial** use. Monetised use required a **Pro** licence.
- Your video was **monetised from publish** (10 Sep) — so on the terms of the Standard licence alone, the claim wasn't a mistake.
- However, Glasswork Support (Priya, 15 Sep, ticket GW-SUP-55120) **retroactively extended your licence** to cover monetised use on this channel, at no extra cost, and explicitly said: *"If a content-matching claim is raised on a video that uses the track, reply to this email with the case reference and we will ask our administrator to release it."*

That extension is your real basis for saying the removal was a mistake — not the original purchase alone. It doesn't look like that extension was ever put in front of Northline Rights before they rejected your appeal and filed the removal request. That's the gap that makes this a mistake/misidentification, not just "I paid for it."

**Before filing a legal counter-notice, consider taking Glasswork up on their offer** (support@glasswork.audio, referencing case CS-CLM-91077 and ticket GW-SUP-55120) to ask Northline Rights to retract. That route can restore the video and clear the strike *without* the counter-notice's legal exposure (consenting to jurisdiction, risk the claimant sues you within 10 business days). Doing both in parallel is reasonable, but retraction is the lower-risk path if it works.

## Why I haven't marked this "ready to send"

A DMCA counter-notice is a sworn legal statement with real consequences — it can prompt the claimant to sue you within 10 business days rather than let the video be restored. Two things stand in the way of sending this as-is:

1. **Missing required legal elements**: physical address, phone number, video URL, and your signature are all mandatory parts of a valid counter-notice — I don't have these and shouldn't guess at them.
2. **This is a genuine legal filing, not just a form letter.** Given the licensing complexity here (Standard vs. Pro terms, a retroactive extension from the licensor, and the fact that Northline Rights administers claims *on Glasswork's behalf* — meaning Glasswork may be able to instruct them directly), you'd benefit from either (a) trying the Glasswork retraction route first, since it's faster and carries no litigation risk, and/or (b) having a lawyer or your platform's legal help review the counter-notice before you sign and submit it, given the jurisdiction-consent and perjury language.
