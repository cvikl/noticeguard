## Analysis

Your documents build a coherent, well-supported case for a DMCA counter-notice.

**What a counter-notice requires:** a good-faith statement that the material was removed as a result of **mistake or misidentification**, plus identifying details, consent to jurisdiction, and a signature.

**Does your evidence support "mistake or misidentification"?** Yes, on several independent points:

1. **You hold a valid, in-force licence for the exact matched work.**
   - Receipt + Licence Certificate (GA-CERT-08231) show you bought the **Pro** licence for *Glasslight* (Catalogue ID GA-TRK-0412) on **19 August 2025**.
   - Your video was published **10 September 2025** — after the licence began, so it was in force at publication (§6.1/§2.1 of the certificate: "perpetual for content published while the licence is in force").

2. **The licence explicitly covers your use case.**
   - Terms v3 §4.2: Pro licence permits use "as background music in video content, including monetised video." Your video is a 42-minute synth set with the track used as background music, monetised at publish. This is squarely within the grant. Nothing here is "redistribution as a standalone audio file" (the one carved-out excluded use).

3. **The claimant is the licensor's own designated administrator.**
   - The track page (glasswork.audio/tracks/glasslight) states: "Content matching and claims for this track are administered by **Northline Rights** on behalf of Glasswork Audio." Northline Rights is the exact claimant in your case. So the claim didn't come from an unrelated third party asserting independent rights — it came from the rights-holder's own administrator, against a track you licensed from that same rights-holder.

4. **The licence certificate itself anticipates this exact situation.**
   - §5.1: "If a claim is raised on content covered by this licence, the Licensee may present this certificate to the platform or to the claim administrator." This is direct documentary support that a claim on licensed material is expected to be resolved by presenting the certificate — implying such claims are understood to be erroneous when a valid licence exists.

5. **Timeline is clean.** No version mismatch or lapsed-licence issue: Terms v3 has been in effect since 1 June 2025, your purchase (19 Aug) and the current terms (checked 3 June) are the same version, so there's no "which terms applied" ambiguity.

**Gaps to be aware of (not blocking, but worth doing before you file):**
- Nothing in the record shows you *submitted the licence certificate* during the ClipStream dispute or appeal stages — only that you disputed/appealed generally and both were rejected. A counter-notice doesn't require this, but you may still want to try the "contact support@glasswork.audio with your order number" route (per Terms v3 §10.2 and the track page) as a faster fix, in parallel with or before the counter-notice.
- You'll need to supply your real name, address, and phone number for the counter-notice — none of that is in these documents, so you'll need to fill those in yourself.

Given the licence directly covers the claimed use and the claimant is the licensor's own administrator, your documents support a good-faith belief that this was a **misidentification** (the claim/removal failed to account for your existing licence).
