# Review of Your Copyright Claim Dispute Evidence

## What the dispute would need to establish
To successfully dispute this ClipStream claim, you'd essentially be telling Northline Rights: *"I have a valid licence covering the exact audio matched in my video, and it permits this use."* That requires proving three things line up: (1) the matched work is the one you licensed, (2) the licence permits monetised use on your channel, and (3) the licence was valid at time of publication.

## Where your documents are strong

**Licence validity and scope (points 2 & 3): solid.**
- You purchased a Standard licence for "Glasslight" (Catalogue ID GA-TRK-0412) by Lumen Vale on 12 March 2025, governed by Licence Terms v2 (`02_receipt.txt`, `03_licence_certificate_v2.txt`).
- v2 §4.1 explicitly permits Standard-tier use "as background music in video content, including monetised video on social platforms, on channels operated by the Licensee."
- Although current terms (v3, effective 1 June 2025) tighten Standard-tier use to non-commercial only, v3 §9.1 itself preserves your rights: *"governed by the terms in force on the date of purchase. Later changes... do not reduce rights already granted under an earlier version."* So your March 2025 purchase is grandfathered under v2, and monetised use is covered.
- Your video was published 30 August 2025 with monetisation on (`06_video_metadata.txt`) — after your licence was issued and while it was in force. Timing is fine.
- `05_track_page.txt` confirms Northline Rights is the designated administrator for Glasswork's catalogue and instructs licence-holders to contact Glasswork support with the order number — which is exactly the path §10.2 of the terms describes.

## The gap I found

**Point 1 — track identity — does not clearly match.**

- The claim notice (`01_claim_notice.txt`) names the matched work as **"Glasslines — Lumen Vale."**
- Every other document — your receipt, your licence certificate, the track page, and your own video metadata — refers to the track as **"Glasslight — Lumen Vale"** (Catalogue ID GA-TRK-0412).

Nothing in your files confirms "Glasslines" and "Glasslight" are the same recording (e.g., an alternate title, a typo in ClipStream's system, or a different track by the same artist that also happens to be in your video). If they're actually different tracks, your licence for "Glasslight" wouldn't cover a claim on "Glasslines," and citing it in a dispute could backfire or simply be rejected as non-responsive.

This is the kind of detail a claims reviewer (or Northline) will check first, so I'd want it resolved before you file rather than assumed away.

**Minor, lower-priority note:** the licence certificate names the licensee as "Maya Ortiz," while the channel is "Maya Draws." This is almost certainly the same person operating a differently-named channel, but none of your documents state that link explicitly. Not likely to be the crux of a dispute, but worth having ready if asked.

## Recommended next step before disputing
Before submitting, confirm the "Glasslines" vs. "Glasslight" question — check the claim details in ClipStream Studio for a catalogue ID (it may show GA-TRK-0412, resolving this instantly), or email support@glasswork.audio with your order number (GA-2025-03112) asking them to confirm "Glasslines" refers to your licensed track "Glasslight." Once you have that confirmation in writing, your dispute case is otherwise well-supported.
