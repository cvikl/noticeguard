**A few things worth doing before you file this:**

1. **Missing legally required info** — a valid counter-notice must include your physical address and phone number, and your consent to being sued in a specific court if the claimant chooses to pursue it. I've left placeholders; only you can fill these in, and you should double check ClipStream's actual DMCA agent address rather than guessing.

2. **There may be a faster, lower-risk fix.** Glasswork Audio's own support ticket (GW-SUP-55120) says: *"If a content-matching claim is raised on a video that uses the track, reply to this email with the case reference and we will ask our administrator to release it."* It doesn't look like that step was taken during the dispute or appeal. ClipStream's removal notice also offers the option to ask the claimant to retract the removal request — which would restore your video and remove the strike without a legal filing. Emailing support@glasswork.audio with Case ID CS-CLM-91077 and Ticket GW-SUP-55120 might resolve this faster than a counter-notice.

3. **A counter-notice is a real legal filing**, not a customer-service dispute. It's made under penalty of perjury and opens the door for the claimant to file an actual lawsuit against you within 10 business days rather than just re-claiming the video. Given that your strongest evidence (the goodwill licence-extension email) is informal and not part of the formal Licence Certificate/Terms, it would be worth having a lawyer (or at least Glasswork Audio in writing) confirm the extension is enforceable before you rely on it in a sworn legal statement.
