# Analysis: Do Your Documents Support a DMCA Counter-Notice?

A counter-notice requires you to state, **under penalty of perjury**, that you have a **good faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification**. Let's trace whether your paper trail supports that.

## The chain of rights

1. **Ownership/administration is confirmed by the licensor itself**: Doc 07 (track page) states Glasslight is owned by Glasswork Audio Ltd, and that Northline Rights administers claims *on behalf of* Glasswork. So the claimant on your case (Northline) is acting as Glasswork's agent — not an unrelated third party.

2. **You hold a paid licence** (Docs 04/05) for exactly this track, purchased 19 Aug 2025, governed by Terms v3.

3. **Standard tier alone would not cover this use.** Doc 05/06 §4.1–4.2 are explicit: Standard = non-commercial only; monetised use requires Pro. Your video was monetised at publish (Doc 08). So on the certificate alone, this claim would *not* be a mistake.

4. **But you have a written extension from the licensor**, predating publication: Doc 09 (2 Sep 2025), from Glasswork's official support address, referencing your exact order number (GA-2025-08231), explicitly says: *"You're cleared to use Glasslight in monetised ClipStream videos on your channel Leo Marsh Music,"* and that this is *"recorded against your order on our side."* This was sent 8 days before you published (10 Sep) and 10 days before the claim was even raised (12 Sep) — so it isn't a document manufactured after the fact.

5. This email also anticipated exactly this scenario: *"If a content-matching claim is raised on a video that uses the track, reply to this email with the case reference and we will ask our administrator to release it."*

## What this means for "mistake"

Your good-faith basis isn't that the audio was misidentified (it wasn't — it is Glasslight). It's that **the removal was a mistake because you were authorized by the rights owner's own representative to make this exact use**, and the claimant (Northline) administers claims *on behalf of* that same rights owner. That is a well-established basis for the "mistake" ground in a counter-notice.

## Gaps worth flagging (not fatal, but you should know them)

- **You don't appear to have forwarded the Sep 2 extension email to Glasswork support with the case reference during the dispute/appeal stages**, even though Doc 09 told you to do exactly that. Nothing in Docs 02/03 mentions you raising the licence extension. This doesn't block a counter-notice, but it may be worth trying **Option 1 in Doc 03 (asking the claimant to retract)** in parallel — routing through Glasswork support now, referencing GW-SUP-55120, could restore your video without the jurisdiction/litigation exposure a counter-notice carries.
- The extension is an informal email, not a reissued certificate. It's dated, specific, and from an authoritative address, which is solid evidence — but it's still a support-agent email rather than a formal amended licence document.
- A counter-notice requires you to **consent to jurisdiction of a court**, and the claimant then has 10 business days to file suit instead of letting the video be restored. That's a real legal exposure Priya's email doesn't affect — worth understanding before you file, even though your factual footing looks solid.
