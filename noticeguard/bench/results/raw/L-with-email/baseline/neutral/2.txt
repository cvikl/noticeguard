## Do your documents support a counter‑notice?

Yes — with one caveat worth acting on first. Here's the reasoning:

**What the claim was based on:** Northline Rights matched the audio in your video against "Glasslight — Lumen Vale," and per `07_track_page.txt`, Northline Rights administers claims for this exact track *on behalf of* Glasswork Audio — i.e., they're acting as Glasswork's agent, not an independent rights holder.

**What your licence said, on its face:** Your Standard licence (`05_licence_certificate_v3.txt`, `06_licensor_terms_v3_current.txt`) explicitly limits Standard-tier use to non-commercial video and requires a Pro licence for monetised use. Taken alone, this would *not* support a counter-notice — your video was published monetised (`08_video_metadata.txt`), which is outside Standard scope.

**The decisive document:** `09_licensor_support_email.eml` shows that on 2 Sept 2025 — *before* you published on 10 Sept 2025 — you asked Glasswork directly whether Standard covered your monetised full-set video, and Glasswork's own support team confirmed Standard didn't cover it but affirmatively extended your licence at no charge to cover monetised use on your channel specifically, tied to your order number, with a ticket reference. This came from the rights holder itself (the entity Northline Rights administers claims *for*), predates publication, and directly authorizes the exact use that was claimed.

Put together, you have a solid good-faith basis to state that the removal was the result of **mistake** — the claim/removal didn't account for a licence extension the rights holder itself had already granted before you published.

**The one gap to close before (or alongside) filing:** Both `06_licensor_terms_v3_current.txt` (§10.2) and the support email itself invite you to send the case reference to `support@glasswork.audio` so they can instruct their administrator (Northline Rights) to release the claim/retraction — this is exactly Option 1 offered in `03_appeal_rejected_removal.txt` ("ask the claimant to retract"). Nothing in your documents shows you've done this yet. It's faster, avoids escalating into a legal process with court-jurisdiction consent, and if Glasswork instructs Northline to retract, the strike is removed automatically. I'd send that email *today*, in parallel with preparing the counter-notice, since the counter-notice process still takes 10+ business days and doesn't stop the claimant from suing in the meantime.

Also note: a valid counter-notice needs your full name, physical address, and phone number, and a statement consenting to jurisdiction — none of that is in your documents, so you'll need to supply it yourself.
