## Analysis

A DMCA counter‑notice requires you to state, **under penalty of perjury**, that you have a **good‑faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification**, and to **consent to federal court jurisdiction**. That's a real legal declaration with real consequences (the claimant can sue you within the response window), so it's worth being precise about what your documents actually establish.

**What supports your position:**
- You purchased a Standard licence for *Glasslight* on 19 Aug 2025 (receipt + certificate, GA-2025-08231).
- The Standard licence terms explicitly limit monetised use to a Pro tier (certificate §4.1, terms §4.1).
- On 1–2 Sep 2025, *before* you published, you proactively asked Glasswork Support whether Standard covered your monetised full-set video. Support (Priya, ticket GW-SUP-55120) confirmed Standard alone wouldn't cover it, but **extended your licence in writing** to cover monetised use on your specific channel, "as a goodwill gesture," at no extra fee.
- Your video was published 10 Sep 2025 with monetisation on — after that extension was granted.
- The claim wasn't raised until 12 Sep 2025, so the extension predates everything ClipStream/Northline did.

This is a coherent, dated paper trail that would plausibly support a good-faith belief that the takedown was a **mistake based on incomplete licensing information** — Northline Rights (the administrator) evidently based its dispute/appeal decisions on your Standard-tier licence record, without knowledge of Glasswork's separate goodwill extension.

**Where this gets shaky — and why I wouldn't file yet:**

1. **The extension is informal.** It's a support email, not a licence certificate or amendment, and the licensor terms (v3) never describe support emails as a valid mechanism for varying licence tiers. A claimant or court could argue Priya lacked authority to unilaterally upgrade a licence tier by email, or that it's unenforceable against Northline Rights, the actual rights administrator, who never countersigned or logged it.

2. **You haven't used the channel the licensor told you to use.** Both the track page and the support email say: if a claim comes in, **contact support@glasswork.audio with the case reference so Glasswork can ask Northline Rights to release the claim.** Nothing in your documents shows you did this at any point — not at the claim, the dispute, or the appeal. That's likely *why* Northline rejected everything twice: they had no record of the goodwill extension. This is a live, low-risk option (per the removal notice's own "ask the claimant to retract" path) that hasn't been tried, and it doesn't carry the litigation exposure a counter-notice does.

3. **Stakes are asymmetric.** You already have one strike. A counter-notice is the right tool if Northline still won't budge after Glasswork intervenes — but firing it now, on the strength of an informal email that the rights-holder's own administrator may not recognize, risks a dispute over whether your "good faith belief" was reasonable, and it invites the claimant to actually litigate rather than let the claim lapse.

Given that this hinges on the legal weight of an informal licence variation and a legal declaration with litigation consequences, I'd get this reviewed by someone with legal training (or at minimum, get Glasswork to confirm the extension in a form addressed to Northline Rights, referencing case CS-CLM-91077) before submitting a sworn counter-notice.

**Suggested immediate step, not a counter-notice:** email support@glasswork.audio, reference ticket GW-SUP-55120 and case CS-CLM-91077, and ask them to instruct Northline Rights to retract the removal per their own stated process. Keep the counter-notice in reserve if that fails or stalls.
